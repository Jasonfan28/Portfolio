2026 ◆ City of Philadelphia
Case study

PhillyStat360: Vacant Property Indicator

A model that scores every one of Philadelphia's 520,000 residential parcels for vacancy risk, built to find the empty homes that enforcement records never catch.

Method: calibrated logistic regression and random forest blended into a 50/50 ensemble, validated with spatially grouped cross-validation, and audited for flag rates across poverty quintiles before deployment.

12
2024 ◆ SFU Inclusive Environments Lab
Video and conference paper

Amplifying Voices: Digital Storytelling with Housing-Precarious Older Adults

Three older adults with lived experience of homelessness worked as co-researchers to film their own accounts of what makes their housing work, choosing the locations and shaping the narrative themselves. The video is anchored to photographs they had taken in an earlier photovoice round at Whole Way House, a non-profit housing site in Vancouver's Downtown Eastside. I ran the interviews, shaped the filming content and direction, and built the conference deck, which I presented at the Gerontological Society of America's annual meeting in Seattle in November 2024.

13
2024 ◆ SFU Inclusive Environments Lab
Board game

On the Move

A board game that puts players on a city street as someone living with a mobility, sensory, or cognitive disability. The squares carry the barriers the SWAN audits actually recorded, intersections with no crosswalk, broken sidewalk surfaces, missing curb ramps, along with the facilitators that offset them. It is built for municipal planners, non-profit staff, and community groups, and the lab lends it out for events. I designed the game and wrote its content.

2024 ◆ SFU Inclusive Environments Lab
Exhibition boards

Photovoice Boards for SWAN and Project Sidewalk

Fifteen display boards that take two audit projects to community forums and municipal audiences. Some lay out the method, the study areas, and who took part. Others carry participants' own photographs of neighbourhood barriers and facilitators with their own words beside them. One set covers SWAN, a user-led walkability and wheelability audit run with older adults and people with disabilities across five Metro Vancouver municipalities. The other covers Project Sidewalk in Burnaby, where youth labelled sidewalk problems in a crowdsourced mapping tool and older adults ground-truthed the resulting hotspots on foot. I made these boards, from the qualitative analysis through to the layout.

Interactive

The portfolio you walk through

The other half of this site is a scroll-driven city rendered in three.js, where each section is pinned to the face of a building and the camera travels down the street as you read.

The scene is baked into merged per-material buffers with vertex colours, which takes it from roughly 2,600 draw calls a frame to about ten. It holds its frame rate on a laptop rather than a workstation.
