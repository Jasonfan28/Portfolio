## Overall Impression

This is a good page with a bad first screen and a soft middle. The taste level is high and specific, the writing is better than the layout, and the cartography section is the most convincing thing on it. But at 1280x800 the fold cuts at y=800 and the first thumbnail's top edge is at y=740, the first project title at y=846, the first clickable deliverable at y=1079. A hiring manager with ninety seconds spends her entire first screen on a masthead and a four-cell strip that says "Works 11 / Span 2024 – 2026 / Built for Cities and agencies / Formats Models, plans, maps."

The single biggest opportunity: put PhillyStat360 in the first viewport, and put the four numbers that actually prove something where the content-free metadata is now.

## What's Working

**The plate treatment (page.css:377-383).** Map sheets get `object-fit: contain` inside 14px of paper-tone matting instead of the cover crop used everywhere else. That is a designer recognising that a poster is not a photograph, which is exactly the distinction a cartography-literate reader is checking for. It also preserves each sheet's own composition, so the section proves craft instead of asserting it.

**The section registry (index.html:40-45, page.css:240-252).** A plan-set drawing register borrowed exactly. It is the most content-specific typography on the page and it does real work, because it tells the reader the collection is ordered and finite, which is what keeps a scan from feeling infinite.

**The item copy.** Every description is two to four declarative sentences naming a method, a number, and a contribution. "18,864 taxlots after screening floodway, steep slope, and 1,255 historic structures" (index.html:114). "I built the reproducible R pipelines behind the occupational and industrial analysis" (index.html:79). No adjectives, honest team credit, nothing invented. This is the hardest part of a portfolio and it is done better here than the layout is.

### [P2] The page has no landmarks, no sections, and no usable touch targets

**Why it matters.** Zero `<main>`, zero `<section>`, zero `<footer>`, and no skip link. All eleven works sit outside any landmark. The three section registries are `<div class="sec-head">` (index.html:40, 185, 228), so a screen-reader user gets thirteen flat headings with no way to know that two are map posters and one is the site itself. On mobile, all 23 interactive elements are under 44px tall: topbar links measure 16.3px, `.item-links` anchors 31.2px, the footer email 12px, and the buttons miss by 1.7px.

**Fix.** Wrap each block in `<section aria-labelledby="…">`, promote `.sec-head` to a real `<h2>`, demote entry titles one level, wrap the content in `<main>` and the foot in `<footer>`, and add a skip link. Drop the redundant `aria-label` from each `.media` anchor so the `alt` is announced. Raise `.topbar nav a` and `.item-links a` to a 44px minimum hit area with padding at 375px. Split `§ 01` into two chunks of four or fewer. The natural cut is models and pipelines (01, 03, 04, 07) against plans and policy (02, 05, 06, 08), which is a more useful signal to a hiring manager than "Data & Analysis" anyway.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden index.html`, then `/impeccable adapt index.html`

## Minor Observations

- `.feature .item-desc` (page.css:339) and `.colophon .item-desc` (page.css:419) set no `margin-top` while `.card`/`.plate` set 0.5rem, so the two largest entries have the tightest title-to-body spacing on the page.
- The CTA band constrains its paragraph to 54ch and leaves the entire right half of an 1180px band empty. An availability line, a portrait, or the resume belongs there.
- The colophon SVG is the weakest image on a page of strong ones. Two flat grey walls and an empty road, against ten photographs of real work. Either add pinned building faces and a figure for scale, or use a still frame from explore.html.
- Dead CSS: `.work-row` and `.work-links` (page.css:463-464) have media-query overrides, no base rules, and appear in no HTML file in the repo.
- No print stylesheet. Hiring managers print candidate pages more than the web assumes.
- `<title>` and `<h1>` disagree about the pitch. The tab, the history entry, and the search result all get the generic one.
- `.eyebrow` repeats the name that is already in the topbar 60px above it and in the `<title>`. Three statements of the name before any work.
- Hover `transform: scale(1.035)` on `.media img` resamples map thumbnails off their native pixel grid. On a cartographer's page, zooming a map slightly degrades the exact thing being judged.
- Credit where due: `prefers-reduced-motion` is handled (page.css:469), all 33 new-tab links carry `rel="noopener"`, there are no console errors, no 404s, and no horizontal overflow at 375px.
- House voice holds. No em dashes and no semicolons in the prose. The marks in "2024 – 2026" and "01 — 08" are numeric-range typography, not punctuation.

## Questions to Consider

1. Eleven works, one page, and no way to see all eleven titles at once. The `§ 01 / 01 — 08` registry announces an index, so why does the page never actually print one, and instead make a triage reader scroll 4,437px to learn what the set contains?
2. The 3D scene gets six links, a numbered slot, its own section, and the largest illustration on the page, while the Bristol plan gets one link. Product principle 4 says the scene earns entry eleven or it does not belong. It has entry eleven. Why does it also get the topbar, the standfirst, and a CTA button?
3. The plate treatment proves you know a poster is not a photograph. Missoula's poster and the HCMC cover are both sheets and both get cropped like photographs. Is contain-and-mat a principle, or a rule that happens to be attached to the two items in the plates grid?
4. The most credible sentence on the page is the one about batching the scene down to about ten draw calls so it holds frame rate on a laptop rather than a workstation. That is a claim about performance under real constraint, which is exactly the argument an agency needs to hear about a model. Why is it at y≈3,800, attached to the least hireable item, instead of being the thesis at the top?
