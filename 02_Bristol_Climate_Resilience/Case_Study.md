## The place

Bristol Borough sits at the southern edge of the Delaware River South watershed in Bucks County, Pennsylvania. It holds tidal marsh, flood-exposed blocks, and a historic grid that predates modern stormwater infrastructure. Every piece of that inheritance makes it a textbook case for climate-adaptation planning at the small-borough scale.

## What the plan does

The book opens with an existing-conditions diagnostic across four axes: watershed hydrology, flood frequency and depth, heat exposure, and land cover. It then reframes the borough around a focal plan and lays out three strategies.

Watershed repair restores the upstream tributaries that drain into the Bristol Marsh and reduces flash-flood pulses reaching downtown blocks. Marsh protection keeps the tidal buffer ecologically viable under projected sea-level rise. Heat mitigation targets the borough's most impervious corridors with a combination of street trees, green stormwater infrastructure, and paving reform.

Each strategy is paired with implementation geography (which blocks, which parcels), funding pathways (Growing Greener, PENNVEST, FEMA BRIC), and a phased ten-year rollout.
