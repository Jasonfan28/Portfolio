## The place

Spruce Hill is a West Philadelphia neighborhood between the University of Pennsylvania's expanding campus and the residential blocks toward 50th Street. Redlining, urban renewal, and four decades of Penn-led institutional growth shape every chapter of the report.

## What the report covers

The report is a neighborhood portrait moving from key stats and geographic context through history, demographics, education, housing, social and economic conditions, and employment and industries, closing with challenges and opportunities. The analysis draws on US Census ACS five-year estimates, historical research, and local fieldwork.

## The housing picture

Spruce Hill struggles to retain long-term residents. Most occupied housing units are renter-occupied, tenures tied to the student-dominated population typically last 5 to 12 years, and more than half of units are over 80 years old. The report reads these patterns against Penn's institutional presence and the neighborhood's conversion of rowhouses to student rentals.

## The employment chapter

The chapter I led tracks how the Spruce Hill job base recomposed between 2012 and 2022. Overall employment grew about 13.5 percent, driven by explosive growth in the transportation industry, which expanded from 1 reported employee to 1,361 after Amazon Fresh Pickup University City received an operating permit around 2018. Compared to Philadelphia as a whole, Spruce Hill in 2022 shows notably higher shares in transportation and warehousing and in educational services. The chapter discusses what that shift implies for local retail and workforce programming.

## Why it matters for a portfolio

The report shows the full analytic arc of a neighborhood study. It pulls federal data, reconciles it to a neighborhood boundary, builds a narrative chapter by chapter, and packages it as a publication-ready document for a community audience.
