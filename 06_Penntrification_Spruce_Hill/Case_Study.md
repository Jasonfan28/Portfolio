## The place

Spruce Hill is a West Philadelphia neighborhood sandwiched between the University of Pennsylvania's expanding campus and the residential blocks that extend toward 50th Street. It has been shaped by redlining, urban renewal, the 1968 University City Science Center plan, and four decades of Penn-led institutional growth. Every chapter of the report reads against that backdrop.

## What the report covers

The book is a neighborhood portrait organized into seven chapters: geography, history, demographics, education, housing, social and economic conditions, employment and industries, and a closing synthesis on challenges and opportunities. Each chapter combines American Community Survey five-year estimates, LEHD origin-destination employment data, historic redlining maps, and local fieldwork.

## The housing argument

The housing chapter traces a pattern we summarize as "Penntrification": an institutional anchor whose presence raises rents and converts owner-occupied rowhouses into student rentals, displacing longer-tenure residents and reshaping the small-business mix. Median contract rent growth in the Spruce Hill tracts outpaces the Philadelphia average across the 2010-2020 window, and cost-burden rates for renter households sit above 50 percent in the study area.

## The employment argument

The chapter I led tracks how the Spruce Hill job base recomposed between 2012 and 2021 using LEHD Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics data. The most visible shift is in transportation and warehousing: zero private-sector workers in that industry reported in the 2012 vintage, rising to roughly 78 percent of the neighborhood's private-sector job base by 2021. The chapter contextualizes that figure against last-mile delivery growth, hospital logistics, and the classification changes in the underlying NAICS codes, and discusses what the shift implies for local retail vacancy and workforce programming.

## Why it matters for a portfolio

The book shows the full analytic arc a first-year Master of City Planning student is asked to execute: pull multiple federal data sources, reconcile them to a common neighborhood boundary, build the narrative chapter by chapter, and package it as a publication-ready document suitable for a community audience.
